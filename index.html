Research

Developed an end-to-end computer vision and deep learning pipeline for automated fall detection in healthcare environments, using human pose and motion representations rather than relying solely on raw video. The project integrated human segmentation, motion transfer, keypoint extraction, and temporal deep learning models to capture and classify complex human movements. I designed and evaluated both supervised and unsupervised approaches, including experiments focused on generalization to unseen subjects and out-of-distribution movements. This research demonstrated how compact keypoint-based representations can reduce the computational dependence on full-frame video while maintaining strong performance for real-world activity and fall classification.
